Credit Default Risk Prediction

1. Overview

Analyzed customer data to identify factors related to credit default and developed credit risk prediction models using Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and XGBoost.

2. Dataset

Dataset: Give Me Some Credit

Target variable: SeriousDlqin2y

Target: Predicting customers who are likely to default within the next two years.

Key Features:

  • RevolvingUtilizationOfUnsecuredLines
  • NumberOfTimes90DaysLate
  • NumberOfTime30-59DaysPastDueNotWorse
  • NumberOfTime60-89DaysPastDueNotWorse

3. Methodology

Data Preparation

Cleaned data and handled missing values, invalid values, and outliers.

→

Exploratory Data Analysis

Explored data patterns, default rates, correlations, and key risk factors.

→

Feature Engineering

Applied binning and WOE transformation to selected features.

→

Model Development

Built Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and XGBoost models.

→

Model Evaluation

Compared models using ROC-AUC, Gini, KS, and classification metrics.

4. Key Results

  • Key risk factors: Payment delinquency history and credit utilization were the two most predictive risk factor groups, consistently identified across IV, regression coefficients, and feature importance analyses.
  • Risk patterns: Customers utilizing more than 85% of their credit limit had a default rate over 11 times higher than those utilizing less than 15%. Default rates also increased approximately linearly with the number of past-due occurrences.
  • Model performance: All three models achieved comparable performance (AUC ~0.84–0.85). XGBoost performed slightly better, achieving a ROC-AUC of 0.8496, Gini of 0.6993, and F1-score of 0.3365, while Logistic Regression delivered comparable predictive performance with the advantage of greater interpretability.

5. Model Performance

Performance Overview

Index Logistic Regression Decision Tree XGBoost
AUC 0.8495 0.8392 0.8496
Gini 0.6990 0.6785 0.6992
KS Statistic 0.5474 0.5285 0.5433

All three models achieved comparable classification performance (ROC-AUC ~0.84–0.85). At the 0.5 threshold, the models prioritize higher Recall to minimize missed high-risk customers, at the cost of lower Precision and a higher false-positive rate.

ROC Curve Analyst

All three ROC curves are clearly shifted toward the upper-left corner relative to the random baseline, indicating strong classification performance. The ROC curves of XGBoost and Logistic Regression nearly overlap across most of the range, consistent with their negligible ROC-AUC difference of 0.0001.

6. Business Recommendations

  • Use the model as a decision support tool: Combine risk predictions with customer risk segmentation (low, medium, high) to support credit decisions rather than relying solely on binary approval or rejection.
  • Prioritize Logistic Regression as the Champion Model: With performance comparable to XGBoost but greater interpretability, Logistic Regression is well suited for credit risk management and decision explainability.
  • Focus on key risk factors: Incorporate payment delinquency history and credit utilization into credit risk assessment and early warning systems, particularly for customers showing increasing risk signals.

Sales Performance Analysis

1. Overview

Analyzed Contoso's business performance from 2007–2009, focusing on Sales, Profit, products, regions, sales channels, and customer behavior. The results were visualized through an interactive Power BI dashboard to support performance tracking and data-driven business decisions.

2. Dataset

Dataset: Contoso Sales Data

Period: 2007-2009

3. Methodology

Data Cleaning

Handled missing values, removed duplicates, standardized data formats before loading into Power BI.

→

Data Modeling & DAX Measures

Built the data model, developed DAX measures and Price-Volume-Mix analysis.

→

Dashboard Visualization

Created interactive report pages with multi-dimensional slicers by Year, Category, Channel, Country.

4. Key Results

  • Net Sales Amount reached 3,207.87M with 13,642K units sold, generating 1,777.91M in profit.
  • North America led in revenue (1.89bn), nearly 3x Europe and Asia.
  • Store channel accounted for 56.14% of total sales, the dominant channel.
  • Home Appliances and Computers were top categories, contributing ~58% of revenue.
  • Gross Margin improved slightly year-over-year (55.01% → 55.50%), though it varied notably by season and region — North America saw margin drop sharply during Holiday season (52.70%), contrasting with Asia's peak (57.19%) in the same period, suggesting differing pricing strategies or product mix across regions.
  • Volume growth was the main driver of revenue growth, outweighing price and new product effects.

5. Dashboard

Customer Purchase Behavior

6. Business Recommendations

  • Price Impact was near zero, suggesting untapped pricing leverage — consider testing price adjustments in select categories.
  • North America's Gross Margin dropped sharply during Holiday (52.70%), contrasting Asia's trend — review regional and seasonal pricing policies.
  • Volume remains the main growth driver (825M) — continue prioritizing market expansion and sales volume.
  • Store remains the dominant channel (56.14%) — further evaluate Online channel potential to diversify revenue.

Data Analyst Job Market

1. Overview

Analyze the Data Analyst job market and training trends to identify the skills demanded by employers, evaluate how well existing courses meet industry requirements, and propose directions for developing training programs aligned with market needs

2. Data

Job posting: Glints, Vietnamworks

Training course: Coursera

Survey: Stack Overflow 2019

3. Methodology

Data Collection

Use BeautifulSoup and Selenium to collect Data Analyst job postings from Glints and VietnamWorks, gather Data Analytics course information from Coursera, and leverage the Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2019 to analyze skills and technologies used by the developer community.

→

Data Cleaning & Skill Mapping

Clean and standardize the data, then map equivalent skills through skill normalization to ensure consistency across the two data sources.

→

Data Storage & Analysis

Store and query data, then analyze the frequency and proportion of skills appearing in job postings and courses.

→

Dashboard Visualization

Created interactive dashboard to visualize insights into market demand for skills and current training trends.

4. Key Results

  • Most Data Analytics courses focus on foundational knowledge and skills, making them suitable for beginners.
  • There is a gap between training content and job market demand, as employers require a broader and more practical range of skills.
  • The findings highlight the need to better align training programs with market needs and strengthen practical, job-relevant skills.

5. Dashboard

Skills Demand Visualization

The collected data shows that Data Analysis (17.48%) and SQL (12.30%) are the most in-demand skills, followed by Business Analysis (8.09%) and Power BI (6.47%). In terms of industries, Finance (50 postings) and IT (46 postings) have the highest demand for Data Analysts.

Training Course Visualization

The survey of 348 courses covering 5,718 skills shows that 55.17% of courses are at the Beginner level, while only 5.46% are at the Advanced level. Course content is primarily focused on Other Skills (48.09%) and Analyst Skills (43.67%), while Database and Programming Languages account for smaller shares.

Skills Gap Analysis

The comparison between Course Skills and Job Skills reveals a clear skill gap between training and recruitment. Analyst Skills account for 43.67% of course content but remain below market demand (55.99%), while Database Skills represent only 5.02% of training content but are required in 13.92% of job postings. This indicates that current training content does not fully align with actual industry needs.

6. Business Recommendations

  • Develop more advanced training programs beyond the current Beginner-focused approach.
  • Integrate Business Analysis concepts into Data Analyst training to strengthen business understanding.
  • Increase project-based learning to provide hands-on experience aligned with industry requirements.
  • Incorporate AI tools and applications into training programs to prepare learners for the evolving Data Analytics landscape.
